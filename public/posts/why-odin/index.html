Why I Write My Games in Odin

August 15, 2026 — #odin, #game-dev, #systems — 345 words

Every few months someone asks me why I don’t just use Unity or Godot for my game projects. The honest answer is that I tried, and I kept bumping into walls I couldn’t see through.

The Problem with Engines

Unity and Godot are incredible tools. They let you build complex games quickly, and they’ve democratized game development in ways that are genuinely important. But they’re also opaque. When something breaks, you’re debugging someone else’s abstractions. When performance tanks, you’re guessing at what the engine is doing under the hood.

I don’t like guessing. I like knowing.

Odin + raylib

Odin is a systems language with a C-like feel and modern conveniences. It has explicit memory management, minimal runtime overhead, and a syntax that feels like it was designed by someone who actually writes code. The built-in core: and vendor: packages cover most of what you need without pulling in a massive ecosystem.

raylib is a C library for game development that’s deliberately simple. No scene graph. No component system. Just a window, some shapes, textures, and input. It gives you enough to build anything, and little enough that you understand every piece.

Together, they feel like a well-configured terminal: everything is where you expect it, nothing is hiding from you.

What This Actually Looks Like

My current project, fimbul, is built entirely in Odin with raylib. I handle my own ECS (entity-component-system), my own audio synthesis, my own world generation. When something doesn’t work, I know exactly where to look because I wrote every layer.

That’s not romanticism — it’s pragmatism. The time I “lose” not using a pre-built engine I gain back tenfold in debuggability and mental model clarity.

The Real Reason

There’s also something else: joy. Writing a game in Odin feels like building something with your hands. You feel the weight of every decision. The memory layout matters. The draw order matters. Nothing is free, and nothing is hidden.

That’s not for everyone. But it’s for me.
